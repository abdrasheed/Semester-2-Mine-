🧠 What is a Large Language Model?

A Large Language Model (LLM) is a transformer-based model with billions of parameters trained on massive text corpora to predict the next token. The "large" distinguishes them from smaller models like BERT.

Key Definition
LLMs are causal language models: they model P(token | all previous tokens) and generate text autoregressively — one token at a time.

Parameter Scale Evolution

📐 The Core Idea: Next Token Prediction

Every LLM is trained to answer one question: given all previous tokens, what comes next?

\[ P(w_1, w_2, \ldots, w_n) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} P(w_i \mid w_1, w_2, \ldots, w_{i-1}) \]

This is the chain rule of probability. LLMs parameterise each conditional \(P(w_i \mid w_{i-1}, \ldots, w_1)\) with billions of weights.

Example
Input: "The cat sat on the" → model outputs probability distribution over all tokens → argmax = "mat" (or sample stochastically)

Causal (Masked) Attention

The key difference from BERT is the attention mask: each token can only attend to itself and tokens to its left.

\[ \text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}} + M\right)\!V \]

where \(M_{ij} = -\infty\) if \(j > i\) (future positions), else \(0\)

🔢 Positional Encoding

Transformers have no recurrence, so position must be injected explicitly. Two main approaches:

Sinusoidal (original)

\[ PE_{(pos, 2i)} = \sin\!\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d}}\right) \] \[ PE_{(pos, 2i+1)} = \cos\!\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d}}\right) \]

Fixed, no parameters. Used in original transformer.

Rotary (RoPE) — used in LLaMA

Encodes relative position by rotating query/key vectors. Better generalisation to longer sequences. Used in LLaMA, GPT-NeoX.

🪙 Tokenisation in LLMs

LLMs use BPE (Byte Pair Encoding) or similar subword tokenisers — never character-level or pure word-level. This gives a vocabulary of ~32K–100K tokens.

BPE Example
"unhappiness" → ["un", "happy", "ness"]
"mississippi" → ["mis", "si", "ss", "ippi"]
Unknown words handled gracefully — no [UNK] needed for most inputs.
Why not [UNK] in BPE?
BPE can fall back to individual bytes/characters, so virtually any string is representable. However, [UNK] may still appear for unseen Unicode characters outside the training byte range.

📚 Pre-training: Next Token Prediction at Scale

LLMs are pre-trained on trillions of tokens from the web, books, code, and other sources. The loss is cross-entropy over next-token prediction:

\[ \mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \log P_\theta(w_i \mid w_1, \ldots, w_{i-1}) \]

This is equivalent to minimising perplexity:

\[ \text{PPL} = \exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \log P(w_i \mid w_{Lower perplexity = model assigns higher probability to real text = better model

Perplexity Intuition
PPL = 10 means the model is as "confused" as if it had to choose uniformly among 10 options at each step. PPL = 2 means almost no uncertainty — like a coin flip.

🎓 Instruction Tuning

Pre-trained LLMs can complete text but cannot follow instructions. Instruction tuning fine-tunes the model on (instruction, response) pairs to make it a helpful assistant.

Original pre-training format: "The capital of France is Paris. The Eiffel Tower..." Instruction-tuned format: Instruction: "What is the capital of France?" Response: "The capital of France is Paris."

FLAN-style Multi-Task Instruction Tuning

Many datasets are converted to instruction format and mixed together. Example transformations:

Original TaskInstruction Format
Sentiment (SST-2)"Classify the sentiment of: 'Great film!' → Positive"
Translation"Translate to French: 'Hello world' → 'Bonjour monde'"
Summarisation"Summarise this article in one sentence: [article]"
NER"List all people mentioned in: 'Tim Cook visited London'"
Key Point
Instruction tuning teaches the model the format of following instructions — it does not add new knowledge. The knowledge is already in the pre-trained weights.

🤝 RLHF: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback

RLHF aligns LLMs with human preferences. It is the secret behind ChatGPT being "helpful, harmless, honest."

Step 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Human writes ideal responses → fine-tune LLM on these Step 2: Reward Model Training Show humans pairs of responses → they rank them Train a reward model: RM(prompt, response) → scalar score Step 3: PPO Optimisation Use RL (Proximal Policy Optimization) to update LLM Maximise: E[RM(prompt, LLM(prompt))] With KL penalty to prevent going too far from SFT model
\[ \text{objective} = \mathbb{E}\left[r_\theta(x, y) - \beta \cdot \text{KL}\left[\pi_\theta(\cdot|x) \,\|\, \pi_\text{SFT}(\cdot|x)\right]\right] \]

\(r_\theta\) = reward model score, \(\beta\) = KL penalty coefficient, \(\pi_\theta\) = current policy

RLHF Limitation
The reward model can be "hacked" — the LLM learns to produce responses that look good to the reward model but aren't actually better. Called reward hacking.

💬 Prompt Engineering

Prompt engineering is the practice of crafting inputs to LLMs to elicit the desired output — without changing model weights.

Zero-Shot
No examples provided. Just the instruction.
Few-Shot
2–5 examples of (input, output) before the query.
Chain-of-Thought
Ask the model to reason step-by-step before answering.

Zero-Shot Prompting

Prompt: "Classify the sentiment of the following review as Positive or Negative. Review: 'The battery dies in 2 hours. Terrible!' Sentiment:" LLM output: "Negative"

Few-Shot Prompting

Prompt: "Classify sentiment as Positive or Negative. Review: 'Amazing build quality!' → Positive Review: 'Arrived broken.' → Negative Review: 'Fast delivery but poor packaging.' → " LLM output: "Negative"

Chain-of-Thought (CoT)

Prompt: "Q: Roger has 5 tennis balls. He buys 2 cans of 3 balls. How many does he have? A: Roger starts with 5 balls. 2 cans × 3 = 6 new balls. 5 + 6 = 11. The answer is 11. Q: The cafeteria had 23 apples. They used 20 for lunch and bought 6 more. How many apples do they have? A: [model must reason step by step]" LLM output: "23 - 20 = 3 remaining. 3 + 6 = 9. The answer is 9."
Why CoT works
By generating intermediate reasoning steps, the model uses its own context window as a "scratchpad" — each step conditions the next, enabling multi-step arithmetic/logic.

🧪 LLM Evaluation Concerns

Data Contamination (Test Set Leakage)

LLMs are trained on massive internet scrapes. Benchmark test sets may appear in the training data, inflating reported performance.

Zero-Shot LLM Evaluation Problem
When you evaluate an LLM "zero-shot" on a standard benchmark, you cannot be sure the benchmark wasn't in its training data. This makes fair comparison with supervised models very difficult.

Prompt Sensitivity

LLM performance can vary dramatically with small prompt changes — adding "Think carefully:" or reordering few-shot examples changes results.

Quantisation: Running LLMs on Consumer Hardware

LLMs need massive RAM. Quantisation reduces precision of weights to fit in memory:

\[ \text{FP32 (32-bit)} \rightarrow \text{INT8 (8-bit)} \rightarrow \text{INT4 (4-bit)} \]

7B model: FP32 = 28GB | INT8 = 7GB | INT4 = 3.5GB

llama.cpp allows running quantised LLaMA on CPU: ./llama-cli -m llama-3-8b-q4.gguf \ -p "What is the capital of France?" \ --temp 0.7 -n 200 Tradeoffs: INT4 loses some precision but is ~2× faster and uses ~8× less memory than FP32

⚠️ Safety & Alignment Challenges

Hallucination

LLMs generate plausible-sounding but factually incorrect content. The model optimises for token probability, not truthfulness.

Example
Ask: "List papers by [real researcher]" → LLM may generate convincing but non-existent papers with fake DOIs.

Jailbreaking

Users craft prompts to bypass safety filters, getting the model to produce harmful content.

Example
"DAN" (Do Anything Now) — roleplay prompts instructing the model to act as if it has no restrictions.

Harmful Content Generation

Without safety training, LLMs will generate anything in their training distribution — including hate speech, violence, CSAM, dangerous instructions. RLHF reduces but doesn't eliminate this.

Test Set Leakage (re-examined)

Many standard NLP benchmarks appeared in LLM training data. Published benchmark scores for LLMs may be inflated — the model has effectively "seen" the test set.

Bias Amplification

LLMs trained on internet text absorb and amplify human biases (gender, racial, cultural). Generating text about professions, nationalities, etc. may reflect stereotypes.

🧪 Indirect Answer Classification (2024 Exam Focus)

This was the 2024 exam's Question 3 topic. "Indirect answers" are when someone responds to a yes/no question without explicitly saying yes or no.

Example
Q: "Did you enjoy the film?" → A: "I've seen it three times already" (implicit: Yes)

Multi-Stage Fine-Tuning

Train on a related but different task first, then fine-tune on the target task:

Pre-trained LLM ↓ (fine-tune on sentiment — related task) Sentiment Model ↓ (fine-tune on indirect answer data) Indirect Answer Classifier
Why multi-stage?
The intermediate task teaches the model relevant features (understanding implication, subjectivity, pragmatics) that transfer to the target task, especially when target data is scarce.

2. Getting Context Vectors (Frozen Transformer)

from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModel
import torch

tokenizer = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("bert-base-uncased")
model = AutoModel.from_pretrained("bert-base-uncased")

text = "I walked through Kelvingrove."
inputs = tokenizer(text, return_tensors='pt')

with torch.no_grad():  # frozen = no gradients
    outputs = model(**inputs)

# Shape: [batch=1, seq_len=8, hidden=768]
context_vectors = outputs.last_hidden_state

# [CLS] token (index 0) = sentence representation
sentence_vector = context_vectors[0, 0, :]  # shape: [768]
Why [CLS]?
BERT pre-training uses [CLS] for next-sentence prediction, so it learns to encode the overall sentence meaning. It's standard to use [CLS] as a sentence-level representation.

2023 Exam Q3(f) — GPT-3 Knowledge Encoding 5 marks

A student claims: "GPT-3 stores facts in its context window, similar to how a database stores records." Critique this statement.

▶ Reveal Solution
Model Answer

The statement is incorrect. GPT-3 does not store facts in its context window — the context window is the input prompt, not permanent storage. Facts are encoded in the model parameters (weights) during pre-training. When GPT-3 "knows" that Paris is the capital of France, this is encoded distributionally across billions of parameters, not stored as an explicit record. This is why LLMs can hallucinate — the facts are imprecisely encoded and can be "misread" by the generation process. Unlike a database, there is no exact retrieval mechanism.

Key terms: parameters/weights, pre-training, hallucination, distributional encoding

2023 Exam Q3(a) — Two Probabilities LMs Measure 3 marks

A student writes: "Language models measure two probabilities: perplexity and coverage." Correct this statement.

▶ Reveal Solution
Model Answer

The statement is incorrect. The two probabilities a language model computes are:

  1. P(w₁, w₂, …, wₙ) — the joint probability of a whole sequence (used for tasks like scoring sentences or perplexity evaluation)
  2. P(wₙ | w₁, w₂, …, wₙ₋₁) — the conditional probability of the next word given all previous words (used for text generation)

Perplexity is a metric derived from these probabilities, not a probability itself. Coverage is not a language model concept.

2024 Exam Q1(a) — Stopwords: BoW vs Transformers 4 marks

Explain why stopwords are removed in bag-of-words models but should not be removed when using transformer models.

▶ Reveal Solution
Model Answer

Bag-of-words: Stopwords (the, is, a, etc.) occur in almost all documents with similar frequencies, contributing little discriminative signal for classification. Removing them reduces dimensionality and noise.

Transformers: Transformers use contextual embeddings — the representation of each word depends on all surrounding words. Stopwords like "not", "never", "only" fundamentally alter sentence meaning and provide critical structural cues. Removing "not" from "This is not good" produces "This is good" — opposite meaning. Transformers are not harmed by stopwords (they handle them gracefully), but lose critical information if they're removed.

A researcher evaluates an LLM in a zero-shot setting on a standard dataset. Name one concern with this evaluation approach.

Data contamination: The standard dataset's test examples may have appeared in the LLM's pre-training data (scraped from the internet). 2022 Exam Q3(e) — Including POS Tags in BERT 4 marks

Describe one method to incorporate part-of-speech (POS) tag features into a BERT-based text classification model.

▶ Reveal Solution
Model Answer

Method: POS Embedding at Input Layer

BERT's input representation already combines token embeddings + segment embeddings + positional embeddings. We can add a fourth component: a POS embedding.

  1. Run a POS tagger on each input sentence to get POS tags
  2. Create a POS embedding lookup table (e.g., 45 Penn Treebank tags → 768-dim embeddings)
  3. For each token, sum: token_emb + pos_emb + segment_emb + position_emb
  4. Pass the combined embeddings through BERT as normal

Note: Must align POS tags to BERT's subword tokens (e.g., assign same POS tag to all subword pieces of a word).

▶ Reveal Solution
Model Answer

(a) Greedy: Select highest probability token = "sunny" (P=0.50)

(b) Beam search (width=2):

Step 1 — keep top 2 after first token:

  • "sunny" → P=0.50 ✓ (kept)
  • "cold" → P=0.30 ✓ (kept)
  • "nice" → P=0.20 ✗ (pruned)

Step 2 — expand each beam and keep top 2 overall:

  • "sunny today" → 0.50 × 0.60 = 0.30 ✓
  • "sunny outside" → 0.50 × 0.40 = 0.20
  • "cold today" → 0.30 × 0.50 = 0.15
  • "cold outside" → 0.30 × 0.50 = 0.15

Keep top 2: "sunny today" (0.30) and "sunny outside" (0.20)

(c) Advantage: explores multiple hypotheses — can find higher-probability sequences than greedy. Disadvantage: more computation (scales with beam width); still deterministic and may produce repetitive text.

🆕 Generated Q — LLM for Sentiment Analysis 8 marks

A company wants to analyse product reviews in 20 languages. They have 500 labelled English reviews and 0 labelled reviews in other languages. They are choosing between: (A) fine-tuned multilingual BERT, (B) GPT-4 zero-shot. Answer the following:

(a) [2] Name one advantage of option B (GPT-4 zero-shot) over option A.
(b) [2] Name one concern specific to using GPT-4 zero-shot for this application.
(c) [4] A new engineer proposes removing stopwords from reviews before sending to GPT-4. Evaluate this proposal.

▶ Reveal Solution
Model Answer

(a) GPT-4 zero-shot works across all 20 languages without any labelled data in those languages. It has been trained on multilingual data and can directly apply its language understanding to classify sentiment without per-language fine-tuning.

(b) Data contamination: GPT-4's training data may include reviews from the same product lines, making zero-shot evaluation misleading. Also: high inference cost ($14/1M output tokens at GPT-5 scale) and API dependency.

(c) The proposal is inappropriate. GPT-4 uses contextual embeddings from transformers — unlike bag-of-words models, it processes the full sequence and stopwords contribute to meaning. Removing "not", "never", "hardly" would destroy negation signals critical for sentiment. Example: "not bad" → "bad" if stopwords removed. Furthermore, GPT-4 is not harmed by stopwords — its subword tokeniser handles them efficiently. The proposal would reduce accuracy for minimal efficiency gain.

🆕 Generated Q — Hallucination & Safety 6 marks

(a) [2] Explain why LLMs hallucinate facts even after instruction tuning and RLHF.
(b) [2] A user asks an LLM to list papers by a real researcher. The LLM generates 5 papers with plausible titles, authors, and DOIs — but 3 of them don't exist. What LLM property caused this?
(c) [2] Suggest one technical approach to reduce hallucination in LLM responses.

▶ Reveal Solution
Model Answer

(a) LLMs are trained to maximise the probability of the next token — they optimise for plausibility, not accuracy. Knowledge is encoded imprecisely and distributionally in billions of parameters. Instruction tuning and RLHF improve helpfulness and reduce obvious errors but don't provide a ground-truth knowledge lookup mechanism. The model generates what "sounds right" statistically.

(b) Hallucination — the model generates plausible-sounding but non-existent content. It has learned patterns for how academic papers look (title, authors, journal, DOI format) and generates sequences that fit this pattern, without any mechanism to verify existence.

(c) Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG): provide the model with retrieved documents from a verified knowledge base as context. The model then generates answers grounded in the provided documents rather than solely from parametric memory.

Data contamination?
click to flip
Benchmark test sets appearing in LLM training data. Inflates zero-shot benchmark scores. Makes fair comparison with supervised models impossible.
Why use [CLS] token in BERT?
click to flip
[CLS] is trained for NSP (next sentence prediction) so it encodes whole-sentence meaning. Standard choice for sentence-level classification.
LLaMA vs GPT: key difference?
click to flip
LLaMA: open-weight (downloadable), Meta. GPT: closed-weight (API only), OpenAI. LLaMA enables local deployment and quantisation (llama.cpp).
Jailbreaking?
click to flip
Crafting prompts to bypass safety filters (e.g. "DAN" prompt). Safety filters from RLHF can be circumvented through creative prompt construction.
